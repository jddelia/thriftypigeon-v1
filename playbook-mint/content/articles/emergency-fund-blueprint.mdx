Keeping three months of expenses in cash is the dividing line between *panic* and *optionality*. Yet most people stall out after saving one paycheck because the plan is just "spend less". This guide gives you a sprint plan with guardrails so the money appears quickly **and** stays untouched.

## Week 0: Get brutally clear on the number

Start with a baseline so the finish line never feels fuzzy.

1. Add up the *true* monthly cost of housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments, and non-negotiables.
2. Multiply by three. That is your target. If the number looks intimidating, break it into six weekly milestones so you always know if you’re ahead or behind.
3. Create a separate high-yield savings account nicknamed `Emergency Fund`. This is where every dollar will land.

> Pro tip: If your number feels impossible, swap to a 6-week rolling buffer. Cover rent, groceries, and minimum bills first. Momentum beats perfection.

## Week 1-2: Capture the quick wins

Before slashing lattes, take the frictionless wins.

- Cancel or pause anything that no longer sparks value: duplicate software, unused subscriptions, dormant memberships.
- Call providers (internet, insurance, phone) and ask for promotional pricing. Bundle if needed.
- Run a possessions audit. List what you haven’t touched in 90 days and decide what can be sold this week.

Every savings win routes to the new account immediately. Do not let it linger in checking.

## Week 3-4: Automate the bulk savings

Now build a machine that moves cash without asking for willpower.

- Set up automatic transfers the morning after payday. Start at 10% of take-home pay and adjust weekly based on momentum.
- Use an envelope rule for the biggest leaking category (usually food delivery or impulse shopping). Give yourself a fixed weekly allowance and lock the rest away.
- Stack rewards: redirect cash-back cards and employer stipends straight to the emergency account.

Because the emergency account lives at a separate bank, “borrowing” from it requires multiple steps. That friction protects your progress.

## Week 5-6: Protect and celebrate the finish

The final stretch is where people rationalize a pause. Stay ruthless.

- Keep a simple dashboard that shows target vs. actual every week. Visual momentum kills complacency.
- Automate a weekly calendar reminder to celebrate the amount saved. Tie the win to the future scenario it protects (e.g., “This covers rent if my biggest client churns”).
- Once you hit the goal, leave the automation running but re-route the cash toward your next milestone (debt payoff, investing, or a mini-retirement fund).

The more you normalize hitting financial goals fast, the easier it becomes to stack the next ones. Your emergency fund is the confidence engine for every future bet.
